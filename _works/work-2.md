### Context and Objective

Booking.com’s design organisation was growing rapidly across locations, platforms, and disciplines. As teams scaled, so did the complexity of working together. Knowledge was scattered across tools. Collaboration was inconsistent. Onboarding new designers and writers was challenging.

DesignOps was introduced to reduce friction, improve visibility, and make essential tools, people, and processes easier to access and navigate.

---

### My Role

I was part of the core team that established DesignOps at Booking.com and later became the team’s manager. My responsibilities spanned from research and problem analysis to hands-on design and people leadership.

- Led discovery work to identify operational pain points through interviews, audits, and collaboration with cross-functional teams.
- Designed and delivered internal platforms and tools, including an internal portal and UX people directory, focusing on usability and maintainability.
- Managed the DesignOps team, helping prioritise initiatives, mentoring team members, and aligning our roadmap with the wider UX organisation.
- Facilitated alignment between design, research, and writing teams by defining shared practices, collaboration models, and documentation standards.

---

### Key Projects

#### <a id="ux-hub"></a>UX documentation

**Goal**: Create a centralized source for UX content, improving discoverability and credibility of internal tools and documentation.

**Problem**: Information related to UX was scattered across tools, often outdated, duplicated, or hard to locate. This made it difficult for designers, writers, and researchers to find the resources they needed to do their work effectively.

**Approach**:
- Designed and launched a software landing page that consolidated key UX resources. From Figma libraries to job descriptions, everything was mapped and accessible through a unified search interface.
- Improved the internal documentation platform to support ease of contributions and readability.
- Developed a content maintenance plan by mapping documentation ownership across domains, ensuring information stays accurate and regularly updated.

---

#### <a id="ux-directory"></a>UX people directory

**Goal**: Help people find each other, their work, and their current projects.

**Problem**: There was no reliable way to know who was working on what, or where to find related files and documentation.

**Approach**:
- Launched a tool connecting designers and writers across teams
- Included live links to Figma files and documentation
- Improved access to active design work across the company

---

#### <a id="review-playbook"></a>Design & Writing Review and Critique Playbook

**Goal**: Improve collaboration and quality across teams with structured feedback practices.

**Approach**:
- Defined adaptable practices for design and writing reviews
- Helped teams adopt the level of structure that fit their context
- Supported scalable feedback loops and cross-functional visibility

---

#### <a id="ux-updates"></a>Quarterly UX Updates

**Purpose**: Keep the broader UX org informed and aligned without relying on Slack noise.

**Content**:
- Highlighted key projects and changes
- Shared news on cross-platform alignment efforts (iOS, Android, web)
- Communicated changes in tools and migration processes

---

### Impact

These initiatives tackled recurring challenges:
- Making scattered knowledge more accessible and usable
- Helping people understand how to collaborate and who to connect with
- Supporting shared ownership of quality, consistency, and alignment

Ultimately, it improved how people found information, connected with each other, and aligned their work. They helped reduce duplication, support smoother onboarding, and create clarity at scale without adding complexity.
