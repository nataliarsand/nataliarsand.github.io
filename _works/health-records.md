### Situation

Haiti in 2012 was the poorest country in the Americas. The 2010 earthquake had destroyed 80% of healthcare infrastructure. Literacy levels were around 50%, half of children were unvaccinated, and fewer than 30% reached 6th grade. Partners in Health built Hôpital Universitaire de Mirebalais to become the best and biggest hospital in Haiti, providing care for 3 million people. ThoughtWorks was brought in to build the digital experience on top of OpenMRS, an open-source medical records system for developing countries.

---

### Task

Three months after the project kicked off, I was brought in to fix the UX problems the team was encountering. The challenge was designing for extreme constraints: users with low literacy, many who had never used a computer, and a high-pressure hospital environment where every second mattered. Changing the OpenMRS interface was technically complex, so every design decision needed to be validated before committing to development.

---

### Action

**Research & Journey Mapping**<br>
I sat down with Partners in Health specialists and asked them to guide me through patient care from admission to discharge. Ran multiple design exercises with the team and client, generating artefacts that helped identify where to focus design efforts.

**In-Site Testing in Haiti**<br>
Once we had working software, we took it to hospital grounds in Haiti to test with actual staff. The hospital was still under construction, but we role-played scenarios and recorded everything. Key discoveries: many participants had never used a computer before, so keyboard navigation ("Enter + number") was easier to learn than mouse clicks under pressure. We replaced text with intuitive icons and large elements ("look for the heart and click it"). Doctors found the patient dashboard life-saving for surfacing critical data quickly.

**Live Design System**<br>
Created a <a href="https://github.com/glauberramos/style-guide" target="_blank" rel="noopener">live UI style guide</a> (we were designing in Fireworks at the time, so this was innovative) where developers could copy code directly. This enabled faster delivery, faster demoing to the client, and faster testing with nurses in Haiti.

### Result

- Clinicians worked more efficiently and confidently, even under high pressure
- The system was adopted in real patient care, helping save lives by surfacing critical data faster
- Design changes were so impactful that we were asked to roll them out to the core OpenMRS system
