### Context and Objective

Hôpital Universitaire de Mirebalais was built by Partners in Health after the 2010 earthquake to serve over 3 million people in central Haiti. It aimed to become the best hospital in the country as well as a place of learning, staffed by excellent professionals and equipped with solar energy and high-end infrastructure.

But world-class care also needed a digital backbone, a system that would support clinicians in delivering care under pressure, with limited resources, and often to patients unfamiliar with digital processes.

That’s where I came in.

---

### My Role

Three months into the project, I was brought in to help fix user experience challenges. Together with another designer colleague from ThoughtWorks, we worked closely with the development team, medical staff, and Partners in Health experts.

- Ran discovery sessions with nurses, doctors, and clinical trainers to understand real-world workflows
- Facilitated co-design workshops to map out end-to-end patient and staff journeys
- Delivered working prototypes and a shared style guide to support rapid iteration and alignment
- Conducted on-site usability testing in Haiti, adjusting the experience based on direct feedback

---

### Key Projects

#### <a id="ux-research"></a>UX Research and Journey Mapping

We started with empathy and curiosity. I asked clinicians and support staff to walk us through patient journeys, from check-in to diagnosis to medication. We captured critical moments, constraints, and stress points. These workshops grounded everything we did.

---

#### <a id="lofi"></a>Low-Fidelity Prototyping

Our first ideas were sketched and tested before any code was written. With frontend development as a skill, we moved quickly from mockups to live prototypes, validating ideas with the team in Boston and testing with nurses in Mirebalais.

We learned fast, changed things often, and avoided waste.

---

#### <a id="design-system"></a>Design System

To help the team scale and iterate efficiently, I created what we then called a “UI Style Guide”, a live design system of reusable components, where we could evaluate all of our components live to ensure consistency, and developers were able to just copy the code right from there, believe me, it was an innovation at the time (we were designing in Fireworks!). This sped up delivery, ensured consistency, and made it easier for new contributors to join.

---

#### <a id="user-testing"></a>In-Situ User Testing

I planned and led an on-site usability testing once we were happy with our first improvements. We tested everything in context directly at the hospital with real staff. Some had never used a computer before. We learned that keyboard shortcuts were faster than mouse clicks. We used icons, large touch targets, and simplified flows to reduce cognitive load.

We role-played patient scenarios, documented insights in video, and iterated quick to support the pain points we found.

---

### <a id="impact"></a>Outcome and Legacy

- Clinicians were able to work more efficiently and confidently, even under high pressure
- The system was adopted and used in real patient care, helping save lives by surfacing key data faster
- Our design work influenced the core of OpenMRS and was later adopted in countries like Sierra Leone
- We proved that inclusive, localised design can create global impact

This was not just a project, to me, it was a privilege.
